Method

Two machine-learning models are run side by side to map drought-related vegetation stress: a Random Forest (RF) and a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) neural network. Both predict the monthly NDVI anomaly from rainfall, soil moisture, temperature and evaporative stress on a 5 km grid covering 2001–2023. Training used 2001–2019 with 25 km spatial-block five-fold cross-validation, holding out 2020–2023 entirely for testing. SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) then attribute every prediction to the drivers behind it.

Key findings

  • Accumulated rainfall dominates. The three-month rainfall anomaly is the strongest driver in 98% of cells, and deficits depress vegetation roughly three times more than equivalent surpluses lift it.
  • The models agree on where, not why. Their spatial patterns correlate at r = 0.84, yet they share no top-three SHAP driver — the Random Forest attributes to accumulated moisture, the neural network to time of year.
  • Cross-validation picked the wrong model. The neural network led in cross-validation (R² 0.33 versus 0.23) but lost on every unseen-year metric, so spatial cross-validation alone would have misled.
